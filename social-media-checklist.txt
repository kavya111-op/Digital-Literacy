Do’s for Responsible Social Media Use

Be respectful and polite in all posts and comments

Think before posting—consider long-term impact

Protect your privacy (limit personal information shared)

Verify information before sharing (avoid spreading misinformation)

Maintain a professional image (future employers may view your profile)

❌ Don’ts for Responsible Social Media Use

Don’t post offensive, abusive, or hateful content

Don’t share fake news or unverified information

Don’t overshare personal details (address, phone number, etc.)

Don’t engage in online arguments or trolling

Don’t post inappropriate photos or content that can harm your reputation

📄 100–150 Words: Digital Communication Scenario

A well-known example of poor digital communication is when a student posted inappropriate comments about their college professor on social media. The post went viral among classmates and eventually reached the faculty. As a result, the student faced disciplinary action, and their relationship with the professor was severely damaged. This situation escalated because the student did not consider the consequences of posting publicly.

This issue could have been avoided if the student had communicated their concerns privately and respectfully, either through email or in person. Additionally, thinking before posting and maintaining professionalism online would have prevented the misunderstanding and conflict. Responsible digital communication requires awareness that online actions can have real-life consequences.
